# Story-structure review: "Sixteen Milliseconds" (p1-ten-days-draft.html)

Scope: arc, flow, repetition, voice. Numbers, measurements and technical claims were not judged. Fixes only move, merge, cut or re-word the author's own text.

## Verdict (5 lines)

1. The arc is sound and strong: cafe headline, then "laggy the same" (Day 2), "This is breakthrough" (Day 5), "we did it" (Day 7), the turn (Day 8), done (Day 10), one more frame (Day 11), then Interstellar and "Show the world what YOU can do". Day 7 "We did it" into Day 8 "Smooth is not enough" is the best turn in the piece.
2. The main flow problem is mechanical. In seven days (2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10) the author's later answers sit after the "What I decided" box, which reads like the end of the day. Each one explains a timestamp from the body, so it arrives late, repeats that time, and often re-tells the scene. Folding each one into the body at its timestamp fixes most of the jarring.
3. Momentum sags in four places: (a) nine paragraphs of "How I measure" between the hook and the payoff clips; (b) Day 1 describes the team three times (Fig. 2 text, the "How I ran the team" box, the star paragraph); (c) the Day 5 climax turns into a Day 11 data table (Fig. 6b) that, by its own caption, does not show the Day 5 build; (d) "What the phone recorded" puts two data tables between "it felt perfect" and "What I learned".
4. The ending is undercut. "From the lead, on the planets" comes after the closing headline line, so the last thing a reader sees is the lead talking to "you", in another voice.
5. The voice is the author's (short, direct, "SOOO ULTRA SMOOTH", "Haha"), except for the team paragraphs (role names such as mgmt-devices, steward, integrator) and the "In short:" / "In words:" / "is in the Day 5 table" signposts, which read like a report.

## Prioritized fixes (top 15)

### 1. Pattern: answers parked after "What I decided" (Days 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10)

A reader has already closed the day, so these feel like footnotes and repeat the timestamp. Rule: **"What I decided" is always the last block of a day.** Move each answer to sit right after the sentence it explains:

### 2. What I learned, the ending: "From the lead, on the planets. Yes, the agents belong in that picture…"
- **Problem:** this box comes after the closing line "Show the world what YOU can do." It changes voice (the lead speaks to "you") and steps on the best ending in the piece. It is an appended answer to a question the reader never saw asked.
- **Fix:** move it to directly after the Interstellar paragraph ("…our own Edmunds' planet."), as a quote with a lead-in in the author's voice, then end on the headline:
  > I asked the lead whether the agents belong in that picture too. This was its answer:
  >
  > "Yes, the agents belong in that picture. We landed on our own wrong planets: … and you made us measure what a real user sees."
  >
  > And I'd like to end with the headline of fastwork.com itself, …
  >
  > Show the world what YOU can do.

  Drop the "From the lead, on the planets." label. If the lead's answer has to stay short, cut its first sentence ("Yes, the agents belong in that picture.") because the lead-in already asks the question.

### 3. Day 5 · "What I felt that morning is easiest to see in my own swipe…" (through Fig. 6b, "On the original, 26 of the 52 frames…" and the hero-clip caption "The hero part of my swipe from clip 1…")
- **Problem:** this is the climax day, and right after "This is breakthrough" the story jumps to a Day 11 recording of the final build. The text then admits "The Day 5 build was not replayed with this swipe". The detour runs about 30 lines, splits the scene from its own phone check (Fig. 7 sits after the long 12:07–23:33 paragraph, far from the "median of 27 to 12" sentence that cites it), and is told a second time in "My own thumb".
- **Fix:** move the whole block (the four-moments paragraph, Fig. 6b, the "On the original, 26 of the 52…" paragraph and the hero clip) into "My own thumb", directly after "…so the two only compare how each felt (Day 5 table; source)." Re-word that reference to "(table below; source)". Cut the first sentence of the moved paragraph ("What I felt that morning is easiest to see…") so it opens "A swipe through the top of this page is really four moments." In Day 5, put Fig. 7 directly after "…at real Serious heat (Fig. 7)." and add one bridge sentence there:
  > The hero cards leaving under my thumb were only the first of four moments in my swipe through the top of the page. The other three took until Day 8, 10 and 11.

### 4. Day 7 → Day 8 turn: Murphy, "AI slop", and the 10:49 bug
- **Problem:** the emotional turn of the whole story ("For a moment I wondered whether we were making AI slop… Murphy's law… So I kept going") is parked after Day 7's decisions and points forward ("Day 8 has the details"). Day 8 then tells the 10:49 bug twice ("At 10:49 I said the frames were acceptable… But the logo, the language button and the Join button moved up…" and, after the decisions, "At 10:49 I found the bug that turned me around…"). The Murphy definition is also given twice in a row ("it means whatever can happen will happen" / "It means that whatever can happen, will happen").
- **Fix:** rebuild Day 8's middle in this order:
  1. Merged paragraph:
     > At 10:49 I said the frames were acceptable, with a few spikes past 34 and 50 ms. But I found the bug that turned me around. While I swiped up at the end of the page, the Fastwork icon and both buttons at the top of the nav bar shifted up a little. At noon it was still there.
  2. The close-up caption ("The bug I saw, close up and 2x slower…").
  3. The moved Day 7 passage, re-worded at the start:
     > I started to think we might be optimizing the end of the page the wrong way. On Day 7 at 04:16 I had thought v10 reached all our goals. Then I pulled v10 and measured it by hand myself. Many parts were still laggy, and some had bugs. That wasn't the team's mistake. … For a moment I wondered whether we were making AI slop. Then another part of my mind remembered Murphy's law, the way Cooper explains it in Interstellar.

     Cut "(Day 8 has the details)" and the definition after "Interstellar" (": it doesn't mean something bad will happen, it means whatever can happen will happen."), because the next paragraph tells the scene.
  4. The Cooper/Murph paragraph unchanged, ending "So I kept going, to beat the goal for real.", then: "My engineering instinct says that when you are going the wrong way, the best thing you can do is go back to the start and try another way. Don't go down with the sunk cost. At 11:08 I asked for a fresh look at the end, from zero, by agents who read our mistakes."
  5. Then "The smoothest build that week was v14…" (the 15:38 step back).

  The planets callback in What I learned ("All of this reminds me of Interstellar again.") then pays off a scene the reader met at the right moment.

### 5. Day 1 · "Fig. 2 shows what one round did. Who did the work changed almost every day…" + box "How I ran the team" + "On the first evening I wired the team as a star…" + Fig. 3
- **Problem:** Day 1 describes the team three times in a row. The box also summarises Days 2–10 before they happen, which spoils Day 8 ("The big reset came from a new direction") and repeats every later "On Day N…" team paragraph. Fig. 3 (the three-dot thumb illustration) is a "what smooth means" picture that lands in the middle of team wiring.
- **Fix:**
  - Cut from the Fig. 2 paragraph everything after its first sentence. Better still, cut the whole paragraph and go straight from Fig. 2's caption to "On the first evening I wired the team as a star around the lead…" + Team 1.
  - Move Team 0 + the "How I ran the team" box to the end of the story, right before "What I learned", under a short heading such as "How the team changed". There it works as a recap of a journey the reader has just finished. Its first line can be the cut sentence: "Who did the work changed almost every day. Here is the whole team I ended up with."
  - Move Fig. 3 into "How I measure", directly after Fig. 1's caption, with the lead-in "This is what those numbers feel like under a thumb:".

### 6. "My own thumb" → "What the phone recorded" → "What I learned"
- **Problem:** "Under my own thumb it felt perfect." is a natural last beat, but it is followed by a signpost ("The rest of what I felt is in What I learned.") and then two data sections (Fig. 9, Fig. 10, Fig. 10b, Figs. 11–12) before the reflection. The reader leaves the story for an appendix and then has to come back for the ending. "The four replays add up to the Day 11 counts. Where in the swipe… is in the Day 5 table." is pure navigation.
- **Fix:** end "My own thumb" on "Under my own thumb it felt perfect." (cut the second sentence; also move "On Day 11 I swiped the phone myself…" + Team 11 above "My own thumb", so the section ends on the thumb). Move "What the phone recorded" (with its 9 Oct subsection) after "What I learned", next to "Ten days in numbers", as "Appendix: what the phone recorded". Re-word its opening:
  > For readers who want every frame, here are the two sets of recordings behind this story.

  Cut the paragraph "The four replays add up to the Day 11 counts…".

### 7. Opening · "How I measure" (nine paragraphs) before "Here is where the ten days ended."
- **Problem:** the hook (cafe, fake shutdown, wow, "so laggy", "That is what made me want to fix it") is followed at once by a legend: drivers, heat states, build names, samples, bar colours, Chrome grid. The payoff clips wait behind it. The stakes (can AI do this alone, from compiled files, while I keep a full-time job?) appear only in Day 1.
- **Fix:**
  - Before the clips, keep only "My iPhone shows a new picture every 16.7 ms…", the slow/very slow paragraph, Fig. 1 (+ Fig. 3, item 5) and the first two sentences of "Under each clip: one bar per page frame. Grey is…".
  - Move "The counts come from…", "Who moves the page…", "Heat:…", "Builds:…", "Small samples:…" and the rest of the bar-legend paragraph into a short "Reading the numbers" block just before Sources.
  - Move "Day 2's Chrome numbers come from Chrome on my Mac…" into Day 2, just before Fig. 4, where Chrome numbers first appear.
  - Add one stakes line after "…with smooth animation on my iPhone." (it re-uses Day 1's own words):
    > And I wanted to know something bigger: if I build an AI loop and give it a goal and a few clues, can AI get there on its own?

### 8. Day 5 · "At 12:07, after a fast flick lagged…" (one paragraph, eight timestamps) + "By the afternoon, the threads…" + team paragraph "On Day 5 a desk agent…"
- **Problem:** the day's best moments ("told the team I was proud of them", the Join note "LET'S DO IT REAL QUICK") are buried at the end of a log-style list. 21:18 "proactive team" is told three times (this paragraph, the late "By the afternoon" paragraph, the team paragraph). 13:37 "bottleneck" is told twice.
- **Fix:** split it into three paragraphs and fold the late one in:
  > At 12:07, after a fast flick lagged, I asked whether we had ever tested fast scrolling. At 21:35 I asked why we warmed the phone up by hand before every test; Xcode's Serious setting became the default.
  >
  > By the afternoon, the threads I had open with the lead were taking longer and longer to get a response. I wondered why, so I looked at the Claude Code conversation itself. The lead had many messages coming in from agents and was handling every one of them on its own. At 13:28 I asked for tools that keep the status up to date, not agents, because agents spend tokens and make mistakes. At 13:37 I told the lead it was the bottleneck and asked it to delegate better. At 21:18 I took the same idea one step further and asked for a proactive team that doesn't wait for my orders.
  >
  > At 21:39 I accepted the Join-button proposal: "SOOOO ULTRA SMOOOOTH, LET'S DO IT REAL QUICK". At 23:33 I wrote that the best build, v8, was "SOOO SMOOTHHHHH", told the team I was proud of them, and asked the lead to write the story down.

  In the team paragraph, replace "After my 21:18 note the roles became proactive, so owners…" with "From then on, owners…".

### 9. Day 2 · "So I changed how I took part. Instead of writing code, I gave the loop hints."
- **Problem:** the explanation of the pull quote ("The numbers said the page was better overall… a user doesn't watch the average frame…") is the heart of Day 2, but it comes after the decisions box. "Instead of writing code" contradicts Day 1 ("I had no time to fix a page like this by hand"); the author never wrote code. When the explanation moves up (item 1), two paragraphs in a row start with "So".
- **Fix:** after "…felt way smoother than Fastwork, with only a little lag." insert the moved paragraph, ending "…So I wrote "laggy the same" and moved on to the next hint." Then re-word the next opening:
  > That changed how I took part. Instead of only swiping the team's builds, I gave the loop hints.

  Also cut the start of "On Day 2 I stepped out of the chat. From 15:12…" down to "From 15:12 my steps went to the console…", because the 14:19 console sentence just said it.

### 10. Day 3 · proposals explained twice; 22:08 three times; ten digits twice
- **Problem:** "The console was new, and so were proposals: when a fix would change how the page looks, the team asked me first." and, 8 paragraphs later, "A proposal was how the team asked me before a fix changed how the page could look." The PR-007 acceptance at 22:08 is told in the body, in the shot caption ("I accepted "full" at 22:08, without a note"), and through "By 22:08 I had decided six proposals". "every place in the clock had all ten digits spawned" is followed at once by "keeps ten spare digits per place for its roll".
- **Fix:**
  - Open the proposals paragraph with the full definition:
    > The console was new, and so were proposals. A proposal was how the team asked me before a fix changed how the page could look: the problem, the idea, why it should look the same, the risk, and one or more options with before and after pictures to compare side by side. I had accepted the first one at 06:31, with a note: …
  - Start the later paragraph at "The countdown fix from my hint 3 came as PR-007…".
  - Shot caption: "…and my decision, without a note (source)."
  - Hint 3 sentence: "At 15:24 I wrote hint 3 from that look at the rendering: the countdown re-renders all the time, even when it isn't on screen, and it doesn't need ten digits per place for its roll. Three would do."

### 11. Day 4 · "Two things bothered me…" / "After my note at 16:09, a router…" / late "At 15:40… article"
- **Problem:** "At 16:09 I suggested sending each thread to the agent that owns its topic." is followed at once by "After my note at 16:09, a router sent each thread to the agent that owns its topic". The article paragraph (15:40) comes after the decisions, out of time order.
- **Fix:**
  - Team paragraph: "The team built it that afternoon: a router sent each thread to its owner, and the lead woke only for decisions, escalations and messages nobody had answered. The rules every agent loads moved into the repo as project memory."
  - Article paragraph, moved after "…anything bigger comes to me as a proposal." (15:21): "At 15:40, for the first time, I said we would write an article about this, so keep the evidence. That had been my plan from the start: I wanted people interested in AI loop engineering or web optimization to be able to follow what we did and understand it."

### 12. Day 9 · four late "why" paragraphs; 19:44 four times; clip cluster
- **Problem:** the four answers (17:04 order, 17:27 borders, 18:33 flicker, 19:44 phone) sit after the decisions. 19:44 appears in the body, in the team paragraph, in the decisions box and in the late answer. Then six Simulator clips/stills and two frame figures in a row make the "polish" day the slowest stretch of the story.
- **Fix:** fold each answer in after its own sentence, starting each with a pronoun instead of the time:
  - "…the menu second. That is the order a real user meets the page: …"
  - "…many layers on the nav bar and at the end of the page. I looked to understand what was wrong with the original site, and to check that the problem…"
  - "At 18:37: fix it, then propose it to me. The flicker was worth another round: a real user can see it…"
  - "…investigate and fix it first. I drew that line because the team asked me to fix something on the phone more and more often…"

  Cut "At 19:44 I made a dropped phone link the lead's to repair before any step for me." from the team paragraph. Optional: keep the motion clip and drop the still pair for the Join fade and the logo (one picture per flaw), so the day moves.

### 13. Day 10 / Day 11 joins

### 14. Repetition to cut

- Day 6: "At 12:36 I asked whether the team could run on cheaper models…" and the team paragraph "At 12:33 we hit a usage limit… and at 12:36 I asked for cheaper models." Keep the reason in the body and start the team paragraph "By 13:02 a sonnet clerk ran operations…".

Keep both, but tie them: in Day 8 write "As on Day 6, the smoothest builds had changed how the end of the page behaves." That makes the Day 6 line read as a planted seed, not a stray fact.

Keep the bullets (the author's voice) and the card, and cut the "In short:" paragraph, or keep only its last sentence ("Together, roughly 6 to 7 days instead of 8.7, and $3,000 to $5,500 less.") as the line under the card.
