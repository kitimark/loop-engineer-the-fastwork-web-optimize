Clips from the real iPhone screen

These clips come from the iPhone itself. A small recorder on the Mac (our own tool, ioscap) records the phone's screen over the cable. A robot finger gives the original page (left) and v8 (right) the same input. The agents only turn a recording into numbers and never look at its frames; the owner watched each clip before it went in.

Each clip shows two things. The video is what the screen showed. The strip under it is the page's own frame log: the time between the page's animation frames, logged by the page itself.

The screen stalls far less often than the page's log says (OBSERVED in our setup; the mechanism is CONFIRMED in WebKit's source). On iOS, Safari's own process moves the scroll view and the fixed bars, and the page's code only hears about the new position afterwards (WebKit source, WKWebViewIOS.mm, commit 9eb4e2ad). So a slow frame in the log shows up as a part that lags behind the scroll, more than as a frozen screen.
