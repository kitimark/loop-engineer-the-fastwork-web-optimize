## Problem

Where and when: the section "One platform is everything you need" (#platform), about 2,600 px down on the phone and 6,500 px on a desktop. As you scroll in, the page switches that section from paused to playing. The switch costs one style recalculation (the browser re-working CSS for elements) and layout (re-measuring positions).

Today (the landed version, RB):
- Chrome re-styles 99-104 elements at the switch (345 before RB landed).
- Safari (Simulator): the switch's style step contains one whole-page layout, 18.5-20.4 ms in total.
- Phone, before RB: a 29-32 ms style step with 2 layouts inside it (Timeline, OBSERVED).
Ms here say where time goes (attribution), not a speed claim: timing is deferred for this topic.

Why: the pause rule lists the animated elements, and some sit inside nested size containers. Safari re-does layout once per nesting level when they change (WebKit source, CONFIRMED). Step 1 (RBi40p) took the section's own entries out, but the 18 ms layout only moved to right after the style step. A split test traced it to the 4 row-title gradient sweeps ("Document", "Earn", "Secure", "Organized"). OBSERVED, Simulator, 1 run per split arm; why WebKit lays out the page for them is unknown.

## Idea

Three edits, all inside #platform:
1. CSS: the pause lists drop the 32 animations inside #platform that are already held another way (29 that start only when scrolled to, or never run, plus 3 scrolling tracks). Outside #platform nothing changes; no remaining entry matches inside it (querySelectorAll census). What is paused on screen is the same as today at every scroll step (Chrome walk, OBSERVED).
2. CSS: the 4 row-title sweeps leave the list too. That removes the 18 ms layout (Simulator: none in 4 of 4 runs).
3. One JS line: the titles must still wait until they are on screen. The page's watcher (PMsweepGate) already pauses a title off screen and starts it in view. The line marks each title paused when the watcher starts, not at its first report.

The race: without the line (RBi40pt), on a desktop the pause could land in the same frame as the section being marked skip-rendering (content-visibility). Chrome holds style changes for skipped content until it is shown (Chromium source, CONFIRMED). The titles then kept sweeping and showed up 3.4 s out of phase (1 of 2 desktop runs). That the held style causes it is OBSERVED in our probe, cause otherwise unconfirmed. With the line, phase and start time match today within the gap between two loads of today's build (Chrome, 3 runs per build, 430 and 1440, scroll-in and link loads).

Your decision:
- Accept: next, the phone check (T-011) and review.
- Change: blind swipe test on the iPhone first (about 5 min of your time). Or a note asking for anything else.
- Reject: keep today's version.

## Looks the same?

Your note on rev 1: "This change still do animation as the same as fastwork.com website version ? If it illusion that all most user cannot detect is acceptable. let's investigate and verify."

1) Same animation? In Chrome every animation in the section plays, pauses and starts at the same moments as today, within the differences we see between two loads of today's own build. The frames draw the same. Safari on the iPhone is not checked yet (T-011).

Our pass rule, written before the data (P-056c): MISS as written, 2 trips:
- The first load from a fresh server is slow, and in it the title sweeps start 67 ms (4 frames) late. Today's build does the same in its own fresh-server load. (Same run: a 0.9 s fade above the section ends exactly at our read, so it reads "finished" or "running" by chance; today's build reads both ways too.)
- Desktop, scroll 7100: two numbers' edge pixels are off by 1 of 255. Today's build draws exactly those pixels in 2 of 5 earlier loads.
A follow-up added after seeing the data (disclosed as such): 0 differences in 8 + 4 loads.

How we checked:
- Census: at every 200 px through the section, down and back, every animation (43-46 per step): name, element, playing or paused, start time. Phone and desktop width, scroll-in and link loads. Link loads: no difference; the title sweeps start 300-350 ms after the section turns on, in every load of both builds.
- Motion frames: clock stopped, every looping animation stepped through 16 points of its cycle at 8 scroll positions. Phone width 64 of 64 identical; desktop 48 of 64 (the other 16: the 7100 digits). These frames set both builds to the same cycle time by construction, so they prove "it draws the same at the same moment"; only the census proves "the cycle runs at the same moment".

2) Verify: optional. Offered: a blind swipe test on the iPhone (two unlabeled pages; you say if one looks or feels different), about 5 min of your time.

## Risk

- Safari on the phone: UNRESOLVED until T-011. The Simulator shows the layout gone. On the phone, step 1's family once moved a layout without lowering the switch total (block 2, OBSERVED).
- The JS line is not in place before the titles' first style: the watcher starts about 290 ms after the titles are inserted (Chrome, 650 to 942 ms after start), just before the section is first paused. In Chrome that is when today's build pauses them too; Safari not checked.
- The line sits in code of another topic (layout-animations, no active owner); the lead approved it (finding F-124).
- The cut relies on another landed change (the scroll-engine gate) keeping 4 artboard and track classes paused off screen. If that gate changes, they keep a GPU layer off screen: memory, not pixels.
- A future animation added inside #platform must be added to the list, or it runs while paused (cost, not visual).
- Not re-run on this build: reload while scrolled, early jump, fling, back/forward cache (step 1 passed them). Touch, toolbar and rubber band need the phone.
- The frozen frames needed two Chrome flags (software raster) to be repeatable; GPU-raster pixels are not covered.
- The restyle-count model behind earlier predictions missed 3 times; it drives no build until checked.
- The census reads force a style update at every step in both builds, so the skip-rendering race cannot show in it; the phase and onset probe covers that.
