## Problem

On a 1440 px wide desktop window in Chrome with Reduce Motion on, the original page draws the 10 tilted hero photo cards in one of two ways. On a slow first load they are sharp. On a quick reload they come out slightly soft. Only the fine detail inside the photos differs, about 3 % of the screen.

The best build (N: the scroll-engine fix st6-rb2 plus the later fixes) ends with the sharp photos, on a quick reload too. Card positions, sizes, tilt and everything else on the page are the same.

Your pixel rule (PR-010) asks for an exact match under Reduce Motion. The best build shows one of the original's own two pictures, so it draws nothing new. But a quick reload compared with a quick reload differs by 3 % of the screen, above the 0.5 % limit. So it is your call.

## Idea

What we know (de-breakpoints, Chrome headless, 1440x900, 2026-10-04):
- Both versions load and decode the photos the same way: full size, with the same smoothing. Same bytes in Chrome's image caches (OBSERVED); full-size decode matches Chrome's source code (CONFIRMED).
- The soft picture is Chrome's first drawing of the cards. On a quick reload, the original never draws them again (OBSERVED).
- st6-rb2 happens to redraw the photos once, about 0.8 s after load, so they end sharp (OBSERVED). Which part causes that redraw was not traced. The best build behaves the same.
- An invisible redraw of the original's photos made them sharp too, 4 of 4 tries (OBSERVED).
- Why Chrome's first drawing is soft is unknown: OBSERVED in our setup, cause unknown.
- With Reduce Motion off there is no difference: 2 px on one card, 0 % of the screen (st6-rb2).

## Risk

Only desktop Chrome with Reduce Motion on was checked. Phone widths with Reduce Motion (430) show no difference at all. Safari was not checked: the iPhone Simulator tool cannot turn Reduce Motion on.

## Options

### Option keep: Keep the best build as it is: photos end sharp

Change nothing. The hero photos end sharp, the same picture the original shows after a slow first load or after any redraw. The work the best build saves under Reduce Motion stays saved.

Risk: A strict reading of your rule is not met: quick reload against quick reload is a 3 % difference. Why the redraw happens was not traced, so a later fix could change which picture shows (in either direction).

Visual difference:
- Quick reload against quick reload: photo detail is crisper in the best build on 10 cards, 3.05 % of the screen (39,549 px²), largest shade 118 of 255, up to 11,983 px² on one card. Above PR-010's 0.5 % and 2,000 px² limits.
- None of it is flat colour: it is photo texture and photo edges. Nothing moves, nothing is missing, nothing changes place or size.
- Slow first load against slow first load: the same picture (4 px², largest shade 19).
- After any redraw (shown here with an invisible one), the original is sharp as well and the two match (4 px², 0 % of the screen).
- Load counts (Chrome, 1440, Reduce Motion, all our frozen-frame runs): original soft on 13 of 13 quick reloads, sharp on 6 of 6 slow loads. Best build sharp on 2 of 2 quick and 1 of 1 slow.
- st6-rb2 alone, the larger sample: sharp on 8 of 10 quick reloads (in between on 2) and 5 of 5 slow loads.
- Reduce Motion off (1440 wide, st6-rb2 alone): no difference, 2 px² on one card, 0 % of the screen.
- The nav's 'EN' label also differs between slow and quick loads, in both versions alike (83 px). It is a load effect, not the fix.

### Option match: Make the best build copy the original's soft quick reload

Not achievable as a dependable result today. Honest steps:
1. Trace which part of the build redraws the photos at about 0.8 s (one more Chrome check, about 5 minutes, counts only).
2. Stop that redraw under Reduce Motion. If the redraw comes from the work-saving change itself, stopping it may mean putting some of that work back.
3. Re-check the pixels.
Even then, the soft picture comes from a Chrome first-drawing effect whose cause we don't know. It changes with load speed: slow first loads are sharp in both versions, and any redraw makes the original sharp. We would be copying a browser quirk, not a design, and it could stop matching with any Chrome update.

Risk: It makes the page worse on purpose (soft photos) to copy a Chrome effect we can't explain. It may cost back part of the saving under Reduce Motion, and the match could break with a Chrome update or a different load speed.

Visual difference:
- Target: on a quick reload, the photos would look as soft as the original's. Slow first loads would stay sharp, as they are in both versions today.
- No build exists yet, so the shot compares the original with itself: A is its quick reload (soft), B its slow first load (sharp). That is the size of the change this option would put back on quick reloads.
- If it works, a quick reload would match the original to the pixel only as long as Chrome keeps the same first-drawing behaviour (cause unknown, OBSERVED).
