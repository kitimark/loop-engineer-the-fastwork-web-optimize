(OBSERVED, Chrome only; the scene paused case was not tested.)
3. Why you saw it in B and C: Try it's A is the plain old page, and B is the old page plus st6-rb2 plus that option's example (PR-009 and hero-exit are built on st6-rb2). So B showed more than the option's own change.

What rev 3 changes: each option now says exactly what B carries. Whether the face-leaving change is acceptable is a separate decision about a build already on main; I told the lead.

Unchanged from rev 2: the full-screen differentials and the table under "Looks the same".

## Idea

Four tiers. T0 exact and T1 within the old build's own A-against-A noise land as today. New T2 near-identical: only anti-aliased edge pixels or a sub-pixel colour phase, small shade differences, a tiny share of the screen, nothing moved, recoloured or re-worded, reduced motion exact, checked in Chrome and in the iPhone Simulator (WebKit) with a real A-against-A run, and a moving part within 1 device pixel of the original at every frame. T3 is a proposal to you, as PR-009 was. You choose the numbers.

New in rev 2: PR-009 now has its real A-against-A (Chrome, old build loaded twice, same frozen moment): inside the glint and title boxes it differs by 3 levels on the phone and 1 on desktop, as the old proxy said. So the A-against-A for PR-009 holds in Chrome; the Simulator (WebKit) one is still a proxy.

## Looks the same?

T2 means: the same layout, text and colours; only edge pixels a shade or two softer or sharper, or a colour step of a few shades inside a fill. Photos, text and anything that moves are not T2.

How big, on the page and when shrunk (levels of 255; frozen frames; "1x" = one CSS pixel per screen pixel, then half and a third of that size):
- T0 / T1: nothing differs at any size.
- T2, PR-009 glint, phone: a 50 x 3 CSS px line of edge rows (0.024 % of the screen). 56 levels at device resolution, 19 at 1x, 9 at half, 6 at a third. Desktop: 78 x 4 px, 31 / 15 / 10 / 10.
- T2, PR-009 sweep: a colour step of about 10 levels (4 %) inside the letters of one title, 8 x 22 CSS px (0.007 %). It is a fill shift, so it does not fade when shrunk (10 / 9 / 10 / 9).
- T2, countdown roll: up to 5 levels on digit edges, a 28 x 21 CSS px box (0.007 %).
- T3, hero photos (desktop, reduced motion only): photo cards of 212 x 212 CSS px and larger; the skyline photo differs by 6.8 levels on average (118 at most) at full size and still 3.7 on average at a third of the size; smooth photos 0.6-0.9. Phone width and normal motion: 0.
- T3, WebKit hero cards (phone, cards about 80-100 x 100-115 CSS px): up to 130 levels at full resolution, 49 at 1x, 36 at half and a third; the area over 8 levels is 0.14 % of the screen at 1x, 0.08 % at half, 0.01 % at a third.

## Risk

A numbers rule cannot see feel, smoothness or motion at other frame rates; those stay with you. It only works if every case reports the same five numbers (largest shade, share of screen beyond A-against-A, area in CSS px, edge or fill, static or moving) and a real A-against-A. A small tool for that report is a follow-up (not built).

Whether a level is visible is for your eyes: a general rule of thumb says a step of about 1 % (3 levels) in a smooth area is hard to see, but we have not tested that on this page (UNRESOLVED). The pictures are at real size so you can judge. If you want "small on the real display" to count as a rule, say so in your decision: it would add one line to T2 (the change after shrinking to the size shown), not a new option.

Risk: Zero risk of an unnoticed change. The cost is your attention: a 1-shade case and a 64-shade case look the same in the queue.

### Option standard: B. Standard: 24 shades fill, 64 edge, 0.1 % of screen

T2 numbers: fill pixels up to 24 of 255, edge pixels up to 64, at most 0.1 % of the screen beyond A-against-A and at most 300 CSS px per element, plus the other T2 conditions. On whole frames at real size, PR-009 measures: phone glint 56 levels in a 50x3 px line (0.024 %), desktop glint 31 and a few title bits, desktop sweep 10 levels on 8x22 px, phone sweep letters up to 37. All pass. The countdown strips (1 shade) and the countdown roll (5 shades) pass. The sharper photos (118 levels, 1.3 % of the screen) and the WebKit hero cards (130 levels, 0.5 %) do not and stay with you. Try it note (rev 3): A is the plain old page; B is PR-009 stacked on st6-rb2, so B also carries st6-rb2: the avatar-slider change (the leaving face is cropped, not squeezed) and its scene pausing. Only the sweep and glint are PR-009's own.

Risk: A fix with a permanent, static edge shade in the first screen would still come to you. The rule trusts a true A-against-A and a Simulator check; the real phone stays a separate no-worse check.

Visual difference:
- Edge pixels up to a quarter softer or sharper, fills a few shades different, tiny areas
- Never: photos, text, positions, colours, reduced motion
