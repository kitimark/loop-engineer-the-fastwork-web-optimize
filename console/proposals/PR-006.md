# PR-006: Smaller image files: less decoded image memory, but not pixel-identical

- by: fix-image-size
- area: whole
- topic: image-size
- state: withdrawn
- rev: 1
- updated: 2026-10-02T15:36:21.243Z

## Problem

45 photos on the page are stored much larger than they are ever drawn (for example a 480x480 avatar drawn 59 px wide on the phone, or a 1272x1272 service photo drawn 161 px wide on desktop). The browser decodes them at full size. Decoded at their natural size, these 45 images take about 95 MB. More decoded image memory makes it more likely that iOS runs short of memory under pressure and drops the page's smooth-scrolling layers. That is a risk, not something we have seen happen. This change does not make scrolling smoother by itself: it is insurance, not a frame-rate fix.

## Idea

Re-save the oversized images at a smaller size: at most 1.2x the largest size any of 9 screen sizes needs at 3x pixel density. Same file names, same format, the original JPEG quality (capped at 90), so nothing else on the page changes. Layout is unchanged: 0 image box differences over 9 screen sizes and 1204 image positions.

## Looks the same?

At normal size the images look the same to the eye. In a whole-page comparison at 430x932, the A-vs-B difference is no larger than the difference between two loads of the unchanged page (the countdown and the animation phase).

## Risk

Pinch-zoom: zoomed in, the smaller files have less detail (softer at 1.2x the needed size, earlier than the original). The memory gain was measured only in the iPhone Simulator (2 sessions); the real phone was not measured. A future redesign that draws these images larger would need the originals again.

## Options

### Option all45: All 45 images

Re-save all 45 oversized images.

Visual difference:
- NOT pixel-identical. Per image, comparing the same spot with the old and new file in place:
- Small round avatars in the commission section (28 px on the phone) are slightly softer: up to a 109 out of 255 difference on single pixels, on about 7% of the avatar's pixels (client-avatar-1; client-avatar-2 up to 92-103).
- The seller avatars in the tabs (59 px on the phone) are slightly softer: up to 71 (web-developer), 48 (video-editor), 40 (two others), on 2-5% of their pixels.
- Desktop-only service photos change in fine texture (grass, foliage, edges): up to 95 (sound-composer service-feature, 16% of its pixels above 8), 87 (service-ost), 80 (web-developer service-dd-transport), 54 (peter cover).
- 12 of the 45 files were never caught on screen by the per-image check (some reviewer avatars, two logos); 9 more were only seen where the unchanged page itself moves.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peak decoded-image memory (Simulator, images category) | 48-56 MB | 32-46 MB (-14.5 and -17.1 MB) | OBSERVED | iPhone Simulator, Web Inspector Memory tracking, 2 sessions (C V C, V C V), A/A spread 6.6 MB; Simulator only, not a phone or smoothness result |

### Option photos: Large photos only (no avatars, logos, reviewers)

Re-save only the 11 large photos (covers and service images). Leave all avatars, reviewer pictures and logos as they are.

Visual difference:
- NOT pixel-identical. The avatar softening of option 1 is gone (avatars untouched).
- The same photo texture changes as option 1: up to 95 (service-feature), 87 (service-ost), 80 (service-dd-transport), 54 (peter cover), 44 (nam cover), 40 (leobeer cover); seen mostly on desktop.
- 6 of the 11 photos were only seen where the unchanged page itself moves, so their numbers are less certain.

| what | before | after | tag | how |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peak decoded-image memory (Simulator, images category) | 48-56 MB | about -10 to -11.5 MB | estimate | option 1's measured -14.5/-17.1 MB scaled by this subset's share of the natural-size saving (38.4 of 57.0 MB); not measured on its own |
| Decoded size at natural size | 95.0 MB (all 45) | -38.4 MB | OBSERVED | width x height x 4 bytes per file |
| Image bytes, the 11 files together | - | -0.75 MB | OBSERVED | file sizes; 3 of the 11 grow by 3-11 KB each (re-saving at the same quality) |

## Recommended

Option drop: Neither re-save option is pixel-identical, and your bar is no visible change. The gain is a memory reserve seen only in the Simulator (2 sessions); the phone has not run short of memory on this page. If small softening is fine, 'Large photos only' keeps ~2/3 of the saving.
