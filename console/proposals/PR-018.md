# PR-018: End-of-page menu morph follows your scroll instead of locking it (phones)

## Problem

At the end of the page, on a phone, the page takes over your scroll: once you reach the bottom, it cancels your finger's moves, pins the page in place every frame, and plays a 680 ms spring that grows the small nav pill into the dark panel (the 'morph'). Your finger stops scrolling the page for that moment.

## Idea

On touch phones only, the page gets one extra screen of scroll after the last section. The last section (#platform) stays put while that screen scrolls in, and the panel opens exactly as far as that screen has scrolled in, so it follows your finger. When you let go part open, it glides to open or closed in about a third of a second (rev 6).

Rev 7 answers your rev 6 review (fast short swipes were hard to open and close):
(1) I recorded your own swipes on the phone (the two-page test): almost all were under 0.15 s, and only 18 of 51 ended where you aimed. Three causes: when your finger lifts, the iPhone has not moved the page yet (it scrolls on its own a few ms later), so the old rule saw a page at rest and could send a close back to open; Safari's bottom bar shifts the ends by 40 px; and the direction came from a single frame.
(2) Now the page reads your finger's speed while it moves. A finger still moving when it lifts decides the direction, and the page keeps that decision for 1.5 s even if the bottom bar moves the page.
(3) Built on v8 (the current best build), which already has the GPU team's repaint fix. You asked whether v8 alone fixes it: in your blind two-page test on v8 you picked rev 7c, and your recorded swipes agree: rev 7c reached the end you aimed for in 42 of 42 swipes, v8 with rev 7 alone in 64 of 85 (12 turned back, 9 stopped part open).

Builds: A = v8 (the best build); B = v8 + rev 7c (file versions in the fix's README).

## Looks the same?

No. The motion is new: the panel follows your finger instead of a fixed spring, and when you let go part open it glides to open or closed in about a third of a second. Still frames at the same opening match the base in the iOS Simulator while the panel covers the last section (only the running countdown differs, in base vs base too). Where the last section shows, Safari draws its text about 1 device pixel higher (T3, below). In Chrome only the EN language label differs. The pictures are from rev 2's build on v7: revs 4-7 change only motion (no snapping, the settle), not still frames.

## Risk

A motion change: you decide whether the end-of-page panel should follow the finger and settle by itself.
- Rev 7 adds a passive finger-speed reader at the end of the page; it never blocks scrolling.
- Still a bit laggy on hard short swipes, on both pages (your note).
- Most slow frames come 0.1-0.3 s after you lift, while the panel opens or closes; plain scrolling had far fewer.
- That is the panel's repaint, the GPU team's area (they have a one-line candidate for the close). Not timed on the phone.
- Seen once per build: a swipe up out of the end glided away and came back to open by itself (cause unknown).
- In Safari the last section is drawn 1 device px higher wherever it shows.
- Not run: reduced motion in Safari, iPad, a reload while scrolled into the extra screen. It reuses fix-nav-morph's setter and gate.

## Visual difference

- Motion: the panel follows your scroll position, not a 680 ms spring. Scroll halfway and it is halfway open.
- New in rev 7: a fast short swipe goes the way your finger was moving when it lifted, also when the bottom bar moves. Rev 6: let go part open and the page decides at once, then glides to open or closed in about 0.33 s.
- Since rev 4: no CSS snapping anywhere, so flicks on the whole page coast as far as today (measured below).
- Safari only, T3 (over the wide rule by share, not by shade): wherever the last section (#platform) is on screen, its text and edges sit about 1 device pixel (1/3 CSS px) higher. Closed at the end: 0.89 % of the screen over 8 shades, max 77. One screen before: 0.57 %, max 78. Base vs base: 0.
- Why (Safari): the box position is identical to 1/64 px. WebKit gives every sticky box its own GPU layer (WebKit source, CONFIRMED); that this layer causes the 1 px shift is OBSERVED, cause unknown.
- Safari, panel covering the section (m 0.25 to 1): equal to the base apart from the countdown, which also differs base vs base (max shade 224 in both).
- Chrome: the EN language label at the top right differs at every morph value, about 600 device px (0.02 %), max shade 187; everything else equals the base. OBSERVED, cause unknown.
- The page is one screen longer on phones.
- Nothing changes on desktop, tablets or with reduced motion.
