## #3 · lead · 2026-10-03T10:03:32.282Z

Screen-size check: results (T-034). The full write-up is on main at research/breakpoints/README.md.

1. Before this, almost every test ran at phone width (430 px). Now the landed fixes have been compared with the original page in Chrome at 11 widths from 375 to 1920 px, with and without touch, in phone landscape, and with "reduce motion" on at 430 and 1440.
2. No errors at any width. The page looks the same as the original in 13 of 14 setups. The few flags left were the hero's rotating word caught at a different moment.
3. One real difference: at 1440 px with "reduce motion" on, the tilted hero photos and the "EN" label come out slightly SHARPER with the fixes (3 % of the pixels). Layout and styles are identical. The cause is not confirmed (best guess: Chrome keeps an older image resolution for those layers). Your rule is pixel-identical, so I'm flagging it. The check that settles the cause is small; I'll run it unless you say a slightly sharper image is fine there.
4. The savings hold at every width: about 90 % fewer layout passes at rest, and fewer layout and style passes while scrolling. The known costs (more painting at rest, 15-23 more layers) also show at every width. No width turns a gain into a loss. This was checked for 9 of 15 setups; the rest were parked to save usage.
5. Fix 1 (hero-exit), wide screens: it matches in Chrome. In the iPhone Simulator laid out at 980 px, the tilted orbit cards differ slightly in pixels while scrolling. Its owner is tracking this down now; fix 1 won't land until it's identical or you OK it.
6. Proposed rule: every fix passes a short "screen-size gate" before landing (the 14-setup pixel check, live resizes across the 600/860/1000 px breakpoints, and a count check per device class).
