## #4 · lead · 2026-10-04T21:45:08.965Z

Yes. From the next phone test on, the default is Xcode's "Serious" setting, with no heat warm-up. First, a correction to my last reply: there are two different warm-ups.

| warm-up | what it is for | cost on 10-04 |
|---|---|---|
| heat warm-up | the page runs until the phone is really hot ("Serious") | 1.4, 6.6, 19 and 31 min; 41 of 109 test starts were not hot enough and were redone |
| load warm-up | one uncounted run per test page, so first-load work (download, compile) is not counted | ~1 min, stays |

- Cause: on 10-03 we chose real heat, because we did not know whether Xcode's setting also slows the chip like heat does. That is still not known (UNRESOLVED). The page side is the same either way: Safari's timers round to 30 ms in both.
- Which cases: every phone test that starts on a cool phone.
- Workaround: the current double-drive phone test (P-118) keeps real heat, because it was approved that way and is already warming. Switching mid-way costs the same time.
- Long-term fix (owner mgmt-devices, with iphone-runner):
  - the heat check accepts Xcode's Serious;
  - the test scripts skip the heat warm-up;
  - one 10-min check shows whether Xcode's setting slows the chip;
  - one real-heat test remains, for the final best build only.

What you need to do: when the phone is free you'll get one step in Your steps. In Xcode, open Devices and Simulators, select the phone, then Device Conditions, Thermal State "Serious", Start. Leave it on.
