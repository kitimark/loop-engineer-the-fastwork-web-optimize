## #5 · lead · 2026-10-02T15:49:23.229Z

First results from de-countdown (research step done). This is Chrome, phone size, 6x slower CPU, 1 run, so treat the time figures as rough.

You were right: the countdown keeps animating while nobody can see it.
- There are 2 countdowns, each with 8 digits x 10 faces = 80 faces (113 elements per countdown).
- The hero countdown keeps rolling in 3 hidden states: after the hero text fades out (it's still on screen but invisible), and after it scrolls off-screen. The second countdown (inside the closing panel, at opacity 0) rolls from the moment it is set up. At the bottom of the page both are invisible and both still roll.
- Cost while rolling: about 1300-2800 face style updates per 3 seconds, against 30-80 without the roll, and roughly 20-40 % of the main thread's busy time. Most of it isn't the animation code itself: it's the framework re-applying all 10 faces of a digit on every frame.
- It causes no extra layout or paint in Chrome. The cost is script and style work.

The apple.com page you pointed to pauses its gallery when it leaves view (scroll-range triggers) and hides the items more than one step from the current one. That's the same idea as your 3-faces plan.

The proof of concept is built:
(a) 3 faces per digit instead of 10. A math check over 110,000 animation positions shows the same visible digits as today, with 0 differences.
(b) No rolling while hidden, in all 4 cases (faded, off-screen, hidden panel, background tab). When it comes back into view it continues where it would be, so it doesn't restart.

Next: measure the proof of concept the same way, do frame-by-frame pixel comparisons mid-roll, then a Chrome profile and Simulator counts. I'll post those here.

## #6 · lead · 2026-10-02T20:16:26.119Z

Countdown PoC: first results (Chrome, CPU 6x, one run each, counts only, not timing yet).

What the PoC does: each digit keeps 3 faces instead of 10 (24 animated face layers instead of 80 per countdown), and the countdown stops animating while you can't see it (faded out, scrolled away, or the end panel hidden), like the apple.com gallery you pointed to. When it comes back into view it catches up to exactly where it would have been.

Looks identical: 0 of 183 frozen frames differ from the original, byte for byte. That covers normal rolls (down, up, 9->0 wraps, a 4-digit carry like 04:00:00 -> 03:59:59) and the moment it comes back into view mid-roll after a fade-in, a scroll back, or a tab switch.

Countdown alone on a test page (8 digits, 6 s):
- style updates on the digit elements: 3400 -> 598 (a still clock is 70)
- animated layers: 86 -> 30
- main-thread busy time: 433 ms -> 328 ms (a still clock is 42 ms)
- new small cost: about 1 tiny layout and 2 small paints per second, when a face slot swaps its number.

Whole page:
- countdown visible (top of the page): digit style updates 1290 -> 283 per 3 s.
- countdown out of sight (most of the scroll): 2700-3100 -> 15-30 per 3 s, and its animation frames 245-287 -> 0. Main-thread busy time there drops from about 3300 ms to about 2100 ms per 6 s, the same as a page whose clock never moves.

So: while you scroll past the countdown, the PoC removes its whole cost. While it is on screen it saves about a quarter (most of what is left is the framework's update work, which we see in our traces but haven't confirmed independently).

Next: Simulator (Safari engine) layout and paint counts, then the write-up and a land request. Timing on the iPhone waits until the phone is back.
